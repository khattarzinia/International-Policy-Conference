01 / Our mission

Good questions.
Lasting perspectives.

The International Policy Conference was established in 2026 by a group of Harvard students and graduates to bring global perspectives to future leaders early in their education.

Open to high school and collegiate students worldwide, IPC asks students to look beyond “what is the problem” and consider why it persists, how to solve it, and who can help change it.

Through economics, international law, public health, environmental science, and more, students develop research, synthesis, public speaking, negotiation, and persuasive communication skills—with the opportunity to get their recommendations to legislators whose votes influence real-world outcomes in their communities.

05 / Competition standards

Know the
standard.

Brief structure, presentation formats, and judging criteria.

I

Policy Brief Requirements

+

Mandatory Brief Structure

Executive Summary
150 words max. State the problem, proposed intervention, and expected primary outcome in jargon-free prose.
Problem Definition and Scope
Define the problem with at least one quantitative indicator of its magnitude. Specify jurisdiction and time horizon.
Background and Context
Summarize the relevant policy history and prior interventions. Cite at least three independent sources.
Stakeholder Analysis
Identify at least three distinct stakeholder groups; describe each group's interest, influence, and how the proposed policy affects them.
Policy Options
Present at minimum two distinct approaches evaluated against the same criteria: cost, feasibility, equity, and evidence base.
Recommended Policy
State the preferred approach, implementing authority, and a realistic implementation timeline.
Anticipated Objections
Address at least two significant objections with substantive responses.
References
Full bibliography. Minimum 8 sources; at least 4 primary or peer-reviewed.

Academic Integrity Requirements

  • All submissions are screened for plagiarism. A match rate above 15 percent (excluding the reference list) will trigger manual review and may result in disqualification.
  • Use of AI language model tools to draft or substantively write any portion of a policy brief is not permitted. Students certify at submission that all written work is entirely their own.
  • Faculty advisors may assist students in identifying sources and reviewing argument structure, but may not draft, substantially edit, or revise student text.
  • Students must disclose any familial, professional, or personal connection to organizations cited or analyzed in their brief. Failure to disclose constitutes a conflict of interest violation.

Judging Rubric (100 points)

Criterion Description Points
Problem Definition Clarity and precision; use of quantitative indicators; appropriate scoping 15
Evidence Quality Quality, recency, and independence of sources; correct interpretation of causal vs. correlational claims 25
Analytical Rigor Depth of stakeholder analysis; quality of options comparison; soundness of trade-off reasoning 25
Policy Recommendation Specificity of proposed intervention; feasibility; identification of implementing authority 20
Counter-Argument Engagement Substantiveness of objections addressed; quality of responses 10
Writing and Structure Clarity, organization, adherence to required structure 5
II

Video Pitch Requirements

+

Technical Specifications

  • Duration: 8 minutes minimum, 12 minutes maximum. Videos outside this range are disqualified without review.
  • Format: MP4 or MOV, minimum 720p (1280×720). Audio must be clear and free of significant background noise.
  • File size: Maximum 2GB. Submit via IPC upload portal only; external platform links are not accepted.
  • Students may appear on camera, narrate over slides, or combine both; no requirement for on-camera presentation.
  • Submissions must be in English. Students may include subtitles but must present in English.
  • No background music, dramatic effects, or theatrical elements.

Required Content Elements

Opening Statement (≈90 sec)
Identify the policy problem and state at least one quantitative measure of its scale or severity. Lead with evidence.
Policy Proposal (≈2–3 min)
Identify the implementing authority, target population, and primary mechanism of effect.
Evidence Base (≈2 min)
Cite at least two independent studies or data sources. Distinguish correlational from causal evidence where relevant.
Objections and Trade-offs (≈2 min)
Address at least one significant objection and describe the primary limitation or equity concern.
Conclusion (≈60 sec)
State the primary expected outcome and the condition under which it is most likely to be achieved.

Judging Rubric (100 points)

Criterion Description Points
Problem Framing Evidence-grounded introduction within the time allotted 15
Clarity of Proposal Precision of the intervention; whether the mechanism of effect is clearly explained 20
Evidence Presentation Quality of empirical evidence and effectiveness of communication to a generalist audience 25
Objection Handling Credibility and thoroughness of objections addressed 20
Communication Clarity, pace, structure, and accessibility of the video as a standalone presentation 20
III

Finals Requirements

+

Live Pitch Requirements

  • Duration: 12 minutes. Timing is enforced; finalists will be signaled at 10 minutes and stopped at 12.
  • Slides or visual materials must be submitted to the organizing committee by 9:00 PM the evening before the presentation slot.
  • All team members must be present at the podium. Speaking responsibilities may be divided at the team's discretion.
  • Finalists must present from memory or notes; reading verbatim from a prepared text is not permitted.
  • Presentations will be recorded for the competition archive. Recording constitutes consent for non-commercial use by IPC.

Executive Q&A Format

  • Duration: 15 minutes, moderated by the IPC Chair of Proceedings.
  • The executive panel may ask any question related to the proposal, evidence cited, analytical methodology, or implementation.
  • Questions may be adversarial; panel members may adopt a skeptical position regardless of their personal views.
  • Students may consult their notes and submitted brief during Q&A. No external materials or devices are permitted.
  • Team members may confer briefly (under 30 seconds) before responding.
  • Responses should be direct and concise. Non-responsive answers are evaluated negatively regardless of length.

Updated Policy Summary

All finalists must submit a 500-word updated policy summary on Day 2, due by 11:59 PM local time. Required elements: the policy recommendation in one sentence; the three strongest pieces of supporting evidence; the primary implementation challenge; and the proposed metric by which the policy's effectiveness should be evaluated within five years. This document is provided to the executive judges prior to the Day 3 Q&A session.

Judging Rubric (100 points)

Criterion Description Points
Command of Material Depth of knowledge; ability to cite specific data without notes; familiarity with the literature 25
Pitch Effectiveness Clarity, structure, and persuasive coherence of the 12-minute live presentation 20
Q&A Responsiveness How directly and substantively each question was addressed 30
Policy Realism How implementable and politically viable the proposal appears to working policymakers 15
Intellectual Honesty Acknowledgment of limitations; willingness to update positions in response to new information 10

09 / Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Who can participate?+

IPC is open to high school and collegiate students worldwide. Participants may compete as individuals or in teams. Full eligibility requirements will be published with registration for Spring 2027.

What do participants submit?+

Participants (individuals or teams) progress through research, a written policy brief, and a short video pitch. Finalists also prepare for a live presentation and executive question-and-answer session.

How are entries judged?+

Judges assess the quality of research, clarity of the policy recommendation, feasibility, evidence, equity, and the team’s ability to communicate their work persuasively.

When will registration details be released?+

Join the IPC mailing list through the contact section to receive the Spring 2027 timeline, eligibility requirements, and participant resources when they are announced.
